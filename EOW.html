The legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom


Game details


The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom is an action/ adventure video game where, for the first time, you assume the role as Zelda, who partners up with an ethereal companion named Tri. Together, they need to save their land Hyrule from expanding purple voids known as rifts, and find the missing hero Link. Echoes of Wisdom is the 21st installment in the Zelda franchise. On the official Zelda timeline, it follows the string of events after Ocarina of Time where the hero is defeated, placing the game after Tri-Force Heroes and before The Legend of Zelda (original title).

Game design - 8/10


When comparing it to the other recent Zelda titles such as BOTW and TOTK, the art style and game design don’t come anywhere close to how it was. However, it still appeals to a good variety of people and it still manages to make things look good. As for the game itself, I believe the design goes back to the same style as the first few Zelda games that were created, but they take it into a 3D perspective and bring it back to today's standards of game design. The only real issue I saw with it was the quality. The switch sits at 1080 p which, for the case of echoes of wisdom, doesn’t hold up well with what they were trying to present. As someone who played it on a monitor, it was clear where the pixels were which made the graphics appear not-so-great. Although it’s also important to consider that the switch can only hold up to 1080 p so it isn’t necessarily a game issue but a console issue.

Plot - 9/10


Overall, the plot is very straightforward and easy to predict, especially if you’re a seasoned Zelda player. It’s also easy to say that this game could very much appeal to children and it doesn’t aim for plot-heavy lore. Instead of there being a complicated story to the game, they make things simple so it can be enjoyed by all. It may apply more to kids, but that isn’t to say that it leads adults elsewhere, as it’s a game that can be played and enjoyed by any and all ages! It’s also important to note the little lore aspects about the game that originally wouldn’t be there in the first place; hence the tie between the old and the new. Putting the different races into the game was a brilliant idea rather than staying completely true to what it originally used to be. Therefore, while the plot of the game may be simple, it’s good and enjoyable for all sorts of people!

Game genre - 10/10


It’s a Zelda game. That’s really all that needs to be said but of course I’ll elaborate a little more. Any and every Zelda game that’s been put on shelves is, without a doubt, a perfect action/adventure game. With all the monster that need to be slayed throughout the game to the dungeons and fun little side quests that get solved through puzzles or other means, it’s a perfect blend for the genre it appeals to. EOW also takes aspects from TOTK where you build things to craft your own unique experience, which adds more to the adventure part of the game.

Controls - 9/10


Not much to say here. Some parts of the controls left me a little stumped when I would put the game down for a week or two and return to it, but other than that they were very straightforward and easy to navigate for the pro controller and the regular joy cons.

Performance - 8/10


As a whole, the performance of the game wasn’t terrible to say the least, but that doesn’t mean it was great. A huge factor in this game comes from the fact that it’s put on a console that’s almost 8 years old, which makes it not-so compatible with a brand new game such as EOW. Not a game issue, but a console issue. Although I did come across a few glitches. For one, when I 100% the game I couldn’t truly do it since I didn’t have access to all 28 accessories, and I believe that’s due to a save issue which I have had some issues with. The other issue I ran into quite frequently was a few camera glitches where the screen would lag anytime I would freely move the camera around. There were also some cutscenes that caused the game as a whole to lag. This could be a console issue, but I’m not entirely sure.

Advertisement - 8/10


In general they promoted the game through various features, whether that be with television, YouTube shorts, or their Nintendo directs, they found various ways to put the game out into existence. As compared to other games that were released close to it, it was advertised much more than those games. When it comes to the actual advertisements, they weren’t bad by any means and told players the basis of what you would get out of the game. Yes it was a good thing, but it didn’t fully pull in a bunch of audiences. There were several stores that offered special items if you preordered the game, such as acrylic figures or fabric posters, which could compel audiences that would love the franchise or game itself to purchase it. Once the game released, there were several positive reviews from all sorts of gaming critics that gave EOW 8-9/10’s which is great indirect advertisement for the game.

Player/fanbase - 8/10


The Zelda fandom as a whole was very excited to see a Zelda game where we could actually play as Zelda, which is a first for the franchise. While that did spark some controversy with seasoned players, it showed us that a great game can be made from another characters perspective. Older Zelda fans who have played several games in past years will find lots of references throughout the game and tons of similarities to old and new games. Did the Zelda fanbase like the game? Depending on who you talk to, of course they did! On the contrary, they may not have liked the idea of having to watch enemies get slain when they could have been the ones slaying them, but it offers a unique way to fend off your foes which isn’t necessarily a bad thing. Anyone brand new to the franchise will find it a warm welcome into the world of Zelda and what it has to offer. Although I wouldn’t recommend EOW as a start, I think it’s a great game, especially for kids who find Zelda appealing.

Impact - 7.5/10


EOW offers a diverse world to explore and several different races of people to interact with, as is not the case with those first few Zelda games. So while it offers a very similar formula to gameplay as the first Zelda’s, it takes elements from newer Zelda’s to make it a more unique and enriching experience. While that may be the case, the game still doesn’t hold nearly as much impact as other Zelda games have. For example, they managed to keep a similar overworld switch up such as the technique used in Twilight Princess, where link needs to become a wolf in order to progress in the game. EOW uses that same technique through the use of the Still World and how you need to enter that world to do most of the game progression; similar to TP. While it may offer a unique perspective to gameplay, it isn’t nearly as memorable as older titles in the series and it doesn’t really offer a new way to make progress in the game to make it memorable enough for players. However, it did introduce the idea of giving women power in games where that has never been the case and it proves that things can be done in several different perspectives. In a way, it can be memorable to some, but not all.

Music - 8.5/10


The music in EOW generally follows the use of mainly wind instruments, contrary to older Zelda games which follow orchestra ensembles with piano/synth sounds. EOW still continues with that concept but mainly sticks to the use of wind instruments for their musicality throughout the game. The Still world uses obvious synth sounds to give it an eerie feeling to it, like something is “off,” as the still world was crafted and created to be as such. However, when exploring the fairy den/fountain in EOW, it only uses winds for that theme, where in other games it has used a piano or harp sound in the background to give it that mysterious and magical feel to it. As is the case with any and all games, music is used to give the people a bigger understanding of what they’re experiencing; hence why the Still world theme is eerie, to create an off feeling, or the overworld theme, which is used to portray a feeling of adventure and desire to explore. I think the music coordination was a good different from the norm, but it lacked a few things when trying to give players that full experience.

Gameplay - 8/10


As you go throughout the game, your main use of exploration is through the use of echoes that can be created with the use of the Tri Rod. You also use echoes to help fend off monsters by collecting their echo once you defeat them to fight for your side. You also have the ability to use sword fighter form for a limited amount of time, which is similar to if you were to play as link. Since you only have a limited amount of time to use sword fighter form before needing to recharge, you can’t always use it to defeat enemies, which can leave Zelda backed in a corner watching her own little army of monsters fight for her. This has taken away the traditional way to approach fights in Zelda games, although, it’s a suiting fit since we are playing as a princess. EOW also brings in the same idea that inspired BOTW and TOTK, which is having several different solutions to a single problem. This makes it to where each player has their own unique experience depending on how they want to play the game using the solutions they see fit. The dungeons in this game aren’t insanely hard as they have been in past games, but they offer some fun puzzles to solve and scenic places to explore. At least they brought them back, unlike the way they nerfed them in BOTW and TOTK. The overall gameplay is great and entertaining for all!

Average score - 8.4/10
