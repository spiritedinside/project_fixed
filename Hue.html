Hue


Game details


Hue is a puzzle-platform video game created in 2016 by Fiddlesticks and published by Curve Digital. You play as the character Hue, who is looking for his mother in a greyscale world. You progress throughout the game by collecting different colors on the annular spectrum ring, which includes 8 different colors. I would compare it to the game Limbo by Playdead, as it takes the same side-scrolling-puzzle elements.

Graphic design - 8/10


The entire game tends to follow a more cartoonish art style with very simple details to outline the different areas in the game. For example, in the cave areas you can see little raindrops dripping from spikes. In the ancient area, you can see vines, statues, and other things that represent old/classical earth; so on and so forth. In the university section, which is the final area in the game, you also find hidden Easter eggs such as Einstein’s famous equation e=mc^2 and other famous people that were involved in STEM back in the golden age of intentions and technology. The art style didn’t WOW me, but I thought it was created very well for the type of game the developers were trying to put out. It’s especially easier to solve a decent puzzle game when you have a simple art style.

Plot - 6.5/10


Me personally, I am very much someone who loves plot heavy games. Obviously the plot was not the point of this game, but because there is one I will give my own criticism. The whole plot of the game is a young boy trying to find his mother. Throughout the game you encounter different letters written by the mother, which explain the different stories and journeys the mother has had with each of the 9 colors. When finding these letters, the game usually keeps an area free of puzzles to allow the player to observe the color being presented and the story behind it. You also find this cloaked figure who appears right before obtaining a new color on your wheel, and right before you finish the game. Getting farther into the game, you realize that this person is sort of like a guide for you to progress through the game. (Spoilers) when you reach the end of the game, you come to find out that the cloaked figure is actually Hue’s father, who lead you to Hue’s mom. When Hue reunites with his family, there is a heartfelt moment shared between the three of them. If you are a sentimental person who loves to cherish the small moments in life and find meaning in small things, the plot in this game is one you will love. For me personally, I felt that the plot was very boring and didn’t give much to the story up until the end. The only thing that saved its rating in this area was the heart warming event at the end.

Game genre - 9/10


This game can be associated with a few different genres and manages to excel in all of them. While it may not be perfect in these areas, it’s still great. The first and most obvious genre the game fits into is puzzle, as this game is entirely puzzle based. The layout of the puzzles becomes progressively harder as you proceed further into the game. This makes the game a fun challenge for anyone who really enjoys puzzles. It’s easy to say that this game is very similar, in the puzzle sense. to the games that Playdead has produced such as Limbo and Inside. Hue is also a platforming side scroller, which gives the game more umph! I think the game genre works very well with the game they were trying to portray, but I don’t believe it was perfect.

Controls - 8/10


The controls are very simple. The only things you can actually do are talk to people, move around boxes, and jump. That’s it! You can also change the color on the color wheel and move Hue around, obviously. Because this is a puzzle game, it makes sense for the controls to be easy and simple, as it gives players a harder time to figure out how to solve the harder puzzles along the way. They didn’t amaze me or anything of the sort, so I give it a simple score.

Performance - 9.5/10

For me playing it on the switch, the game ran very smoothly and I really only ran into one technical issue throughout the entire game, where my game froze and i needed to reopen the game. If not for that, the performance would be a 10/10. Even when it comes down to the water droplets or the vines and chains moving on the screen, even Hue’s hair runs very smoothly. Overall the performance was great and I barely see any issues that are needed for it to be resolved

Advertisement - 5/10


I only give the advertising a 5/10 simply because there isn’t enough of it! While it did win and get nominated for several awards, it only has one trailer and that’s for the PS4. The trailer that was presented gave a very direct summary on what you will see in the game and how it is played. If there were more trailers, then the advertisement rating would be higher

Player/fanbase - 8/10


Due to the fact that Hue is an Indie game, it’s not very well known, however, that makes it a little glowing gem in the dark. Anyone who does genuinely love Hue has shown that through other means which has given several players a chance to recognize it! There are plenty of fan reviews that explain how the game is “worth your time” and “something you shouldn’t miss out on”. For the people who have played it, they absolutely love it!

Impact - 7.5/10


While the game may not have a big community or following, the end of the game definitely leaves a profound impact on the ones who play it, including me. It’s also safe to say that people who aren’t fans of puzzles aren’t going to have fun trying to figure out the final puzzles in the game, as they are decently challenging. The game leaves little impact, but enough for players to remember the game.

Music - 7/10


Most of the music in the game is piano based with very few strings and synths being accompanied by it. It isn’t entirely the best music in the world and at times would leave me to mute the game due to it annoying me while trying to solve the puzzles. Other than that, some of the music is very pleasing to listen to rather than annoying. If the game had more than just piano based pieces playing in the back, then I would have rated this category higher.

Gameplay - 7.5/10


There honestly isn’t much to say for this. The gameplay is good, if you’re someone who likes puzzles. The puzzle aspect of the game manages to carry over very well. The longing of wanting to find this person and also figure out who this hooded figure is makes the game more intriguing, therefore bringing out the curiosity within the player.

Additional details


I loved the little aspects of the game, such as chains rattling when you hit them or the way water flows off of your head when standing under droplets.

Average score - 7.6/10
